Matching the Right Cleanup Vessel to the Waterway Task

Waterbody size, debris type, vegetation, access and operating conditions should guide equipment selection and fleet planning.

There is no single solution for every waterway

Canals, urban rivers, lakes, ports and sheltered coastal areas present different operating conditions. A vessel suitable for one task may not be the right choice for another.

Define the work first

Clarify whether the priority is floating litter, aquatic vegetation, routine surface maintenance, monitoring or a combination of tasks. Estimate the operating area, expected material, access constraints and required schedule.

Consider the operating environment

Assess water depth, current, wind exposure, bridges, traffic, shoreline access and safe launch or recovery points. These factors influence vessel size, collection method and operating procedures.

Think about the whole fleet

Some programmes may need different vessel capabilities across different sites or tasks. Fleet planning should consider how equipment, operators, maintenance, transport and disposal arrangements work together.

Validate assumptions on site

A site visit and controlled trial can help confirm whether the selected equipment meets operational needs. Use observations from the trial to refine the deployment plan before scaling.
