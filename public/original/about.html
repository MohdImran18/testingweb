Where it began

We started with a simple question: can waterway cleanups work better?

In 2019, a student project inspired by the waste seen along Bali’s coastline set us on the path to rethink how floating debris could be collected.

Our co-founders, Sidhant Gupta and Utkarsh Goel, began exploring a small robotic vessel that could collect floating waste without placing people in the water to do the work. The early idea moved from a hands-on prototype to a marine technology venture, and the first Clearbot Neo was developed as a solar-powered, AI-enabled vessel.

Since then, we have expanded from a single early concept into a family of electric platforms for different operational needs—from the Fetch, Class 2 and Class 3 Skimmers to the Alligator Neo Harvester for dense aquatic vegetation.

Our focus remains practical: bring together the right vessel, mission planning, live camera and remote-operation workflows, and a Smart Dashboard so local teams can monitor work and use operational data to make better decisions.

Our journey · 2019–2026

Milestones that moved us from a prototype to real-world operations.

Our progress has been built step by step: early experimentation, product development, field deployments, partner programmes and a growing commercial fleet.

2019
The beginning

A student project takes shape in Bali

After seeing the challenge of collecting floating waste from coastal waters, our co-founders began exploring a robotic vessel concept. The initial prototype, known as Framebot in the NUS founder profile, marked the start of our journey.

Source: NUSX founder profile ↗
2020
Company & prototype

Clearbot becomes a marine technology venture

Clearbot was established in 2020. The first fibreglass boat prototype was built in December, and Clearbot Neo developed into a solar-powered, AI-enabled robotic vessel for collecting marine debris.

Source: Eco-Business founder interview ↗
2021
Commercial launch

Clearbot Neo launches with Razer

We launched the first commercial Clearbot Neo on World Oceans Day in partnership with Razer. The collaboration combined Razer’s design contribution with Clearbot’s engineering and manufacturing work.

Source: Clearbot’s 2022 year-in-review ↗
2022
Fleet expansion

Our fleet reaches 13 boats across Hong Kong and India

Our operating fleet expanded to 13 vessels across Hong Kong and India. The company also progressed through a seed funding round that included Alibaba Entrepreneurs Fund, Gobi Partners and CarbonX Global.

Source: NUSX business milestones ↗
2023
India expansion

Major deployment activity expands into Meghalaya

We expanded our operations in India, with a first major deployment in Meghalaya. This work helped demonstrate how electric vessels could support large waterbody cleanup programmes alongside local partners.

Source: NUSX business milestones ↗
2024
Recognition & impact

PIER71 Smart Port Challenge winner; Umiam Lake reaches 42 tonnes removed

Clear Robotics won the 2024 PIER71 Smart Port Challenge. In Meghalaya, the Umiam Lake cleanup programme was reported to have removed 42 tonnes of waste, becoming a major example of technology working with local communities and partners.

Source: NUSX recognition & milestones ↗Source: NITI Aayog Frontier Tech ↗
2025
Regional operations

Our work reaches more markets across Asia

Our first operations in Singapore, the Philippines, Thailand and Malaysia marked an important step in bringing our waterway solutions to more regional partners and operating environments.

Source: NUSX business milestones ↗
2026
Scale & investment

US$1.75 million Pre-Series A and a larger commercial fleet

In June 2026, Clear Robotics closed a US$1.75 million Pre-Series A round led by Shipsfocus Ventures, with Katapult Ocean, SGInnovate and M7 Holdings MGS Ventures. NUSX’s 2026 milestone summary reports 26 commercially deployed vessels across Singapore, Malaysia and the Philippines. Separately, NITI Aayog’s August 2026 profile reported more than 100 tonnes of waste removed across India, Singapore, the Philippines and the UAE.
